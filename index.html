The Successor

Care that outlives the carer.

A carryable robot that learns how an aging parent cares for their autistic adult child, helps them every day, and teaches anyone who steps in to care in the parent's way.

    What happens at 21

    When an autistic young adult with high support needs finishes school, the services that structured their days end. Their parents, often in their late 50s or 60s, take over: teacher, therapist, scheduler and, very often, remote control. Everything about how to care for their child lives in one person's head. It is rarely written down and never rehearsed by anyone else.

    5.5 million

    Residential places are often allocated by crisis: a carer's hospitalisation or death, rather than planning. When the parent is suddenly unavailable, their child loses their routines, their calm and their sense of safety at the same moment.

    Sources: AARP and National Alliance for Caregiving, Caregiving in the US 2025; The State of the States in Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities; Milbank Memorial Fund.

    Meet the lantern

    About 30 cm tall and under 2 kg, with a matte, warm shell. It has no face and no screen. Choose a view, then select any marked part to see what it does.

      Built to support people, never replace them

      Consent

      The adult child can listen to, keep or remove any clip about them. Live calls are off unless the family turns them on.

      Safety

      The parent marks every routine as "can run alone" or "needs a person". Bathing, and anything with a fall or scald risk, always needs a person present.

      Privacy

      No always-on camera, no face, no cloud subscription. The lantern learns from taps and what the parent chooses to record.

      Ownership

      The lantern belongs to the adult child. When they move, it moves with them, and so does the care.
